This page documents my continuous learning journey and provides honest,
experience-based guidance for professionals selecting cybersecurity courses.

Course completion alone is not the objective. Whenever possible, each learning

---

## 🧭 Learning Philosophy

My learning approach follows five principles:

1. **Learn with a purpose**  
   Each course should address a specific skill gap or support a concrete
   technical project.

2. **Build while learning**  
   Theory is reinforced through code, investigation, documentation, detection,
   threat modelling, or intelligence production.

3. **Review critically**  
   A completed course is not automatically a recommended course. Reviews
   distinguish strengths, limitations, target audience, technical depth, and
   vendor orientation.

4. **Prefer evidence over marketing**  
   Course descriptions are compared with the actual learning experience.

5. **Transform learning into reusable knowledge**  
   Stable notes, templates, methodologies, and project outputs are published
   when they can help other practitioners.

---

## 📊 Review Methodology

Each course may be assessed across the following dimensions:

| Dimension | What I evaluate |
|---|---|
| Course organization | Logical structure and progression |
| Accessibility | Clarity for the intended audience |
| Technical depth | Depth beyond definitions and high-level concepts |
| Practical application | Labs, exercises, demonstrations, or projects |
| Methodological depth | Analytical methods, evidence, and repeatability |
| Vendor neutrality | Balance between transferable concepts and product promotion |
| Currency | Relevance of technologies, methods, and examples |
| Target audience fit | Whether the advertised level matches the actual content |
| Career value | Skills or knowledge that can be demonstrated professionally |
| Next-step value | How well the course prepares learners for deeper work |

The assessments represent my professional opinion based on my own starting
level and experience. A course that is too introductory for an experienced
analyst may still be valuable for a beginner.

### Quick Verdict

A broad introduction to Cyber Threat Intelligence and its relationship with
SOC operations, Microsoft security services, MITRE ATT&CK, threat actors,
threat intelligence platforms, and the creation of a CTI program.

The course is accessible and well organized for learners discovering CTI.
However, experienced SOC or CTI practitioners may find much of the content
introductory. The strong Microsoft and Azure orientation is useful for
organizations operating in that ecosystem, but limits the vendor-neutral
depth of the course.

## Course Scope

The course covers a wide range of subjects:

- SOC fundamentals
- Microsoft Azure fundamentals
- Zero Trust fundamentals
- Intelligence and Cyber Threat Intelligence
- CTI-related frameworks
- MITRE ATT&CK
- Threat actors and Advanced Persistent Threats
- CTI tools
- Threat intelligence platforms
- Artificial Intelligence applied to CTI
- MISP deployment concepts on Microsoft Azure
- APT41 research using MITRE ATT&CK
- CTI integration with Microsoft Sentinel
- Fundamental elements of building a CTI program

## Case Studies

The course includes four main case-study areas:

1. **MISP on Azure**  
   A high-level introduction to implementing a threat intelligence platform
   in the Microsoft Azure ecosystem.

2. **Researching APT41 with MITRE ATT&CK**  
   An introduction to using ATT&CK to research and organize adversary TTPs.

3. **Leveraging CTI in Microsoft Sentinel**  
   Examples of operationalizing threat intelligence through Microsoft
   Sentinel.

4. **Building a CTI Program**  
   A high-level blueprint covering fundamental considerations for creating a
   CTI capability.

## Strengths

- Clear and logically organized progression
- Accessible introduction for professionals entering CTI
- Useful overview of how CTI connects with SOC operations
- Good introductory coverage of MITRE ATT&CK concepts
- Relevant examples for organizations using Microsoft Sentinel and Azure
- Multiple case studies illustrating possible CTI applications
- Useful high-level introduction to CTI program development
- Broad overview of common CTI tools and platforms

## Limitations

- Many sections remain introductory rather than analytical
- Limited depth on structured analytic techniques
- Limited coverage of source evaluation and evidence grading
- Limited treatment of intelligence confidence and uncertainty
- OSINT tradecraft is not explored in sufficient depth
- Threat actor attribution and clustering require deeper treatment
- Tool coverage remains primarily demonstrative
- Hands-on investigation practice is limited
- The breadth of the curriculum reduces the depth of individual subjects
- Experienced CTI practitioners may already know much of the material

## Microsoft and Azure Orientation

The Microsoft focus is not inherently negative.

For analysts working with Azure, Microsoft Sentinel, Defender, or Microsoft
365, the examples may be directly relevant. The MISP-on-Azure and
Sentinel-related case studies also provide a recognizable enterprise context.

However, learners seeking a vendor-neutral CTI methodology should complement
this course with additional work on:

- structured intelligence analysis
- source evaluation and evidence grading
- OSINT investigation tradecraft
- STIX and TAXII
- MISP beyond a cloud-deployment overview
- threat actor clustering
- attribution confidence
- intelligence requirements
- collection management
- analytical writing
- defensive recommendations

## Artificial Intelligence Content

The course contains AI-related material and discusses the intersection between
Artificial Intelligence and Cyber Threat Intelligence.

This provides an introduction to possible AI-assisted CTI use cases. However,
responsible operational use should also address:

- source validation
- evidence retention
- hallucination control
- reproducibility
- human analyst validation
- prompt injection risks
- sensitive-data handling
- explainable analytical workflows
- separation of extraction, inference, and final assessment

## Best Suited For

- Cybersecurity professionals discovering CTI
- SOC analysts moving toward threat intelligence
- Security professionals working in the Microsoft ecosystem
- Learners seeking a structured CTI overview
- Professionals wanting an introductory view of CTI program development
- Analysts who want to understand how CTI may connect with Microsoft Sentinel

## Less Suited For

- Experienced CTI analysts seeking advanced tradecraft
- Analysts looking for deep OSINT investigation training
- Learners seeking structured analytic techniques
- Professionals expecting advanced threat actor attribution
- Learners seeking extensive hands-on CTI engineering
- Analysts seeking detailed source reliability and confidence frameworks
- Practitioners seeking an advanced vulnerability-intelligence methodology

## Key Takeaway

This course is best treated as a structured introduction and orientation
resource rather than an advanced CTI practitioner programme.

Its strongest value lies in connecting CTI concepts with SOC operations and
the Microsoft ecosystem. Its principal limitation is the lack of depth in
advanced analytical methods, OSINT tradecraft, evidence evaluation, and
hands-on intelligence production.
